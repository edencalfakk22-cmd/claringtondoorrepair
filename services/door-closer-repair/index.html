FAQs

Door Closer Repair & Replacement — your questions

Our fire door does not latch on its own — how urgent is that in Ontario?

Treat it as urgent. Ontario's Fire Code requires the closers on fire separations to be kept in operating condition so the door closes and latches unaided, and a door that is propped or fails to latch is a serious finding on an inspection. It is also the difference between a contained fire and one that spreads down a corridor.

Can a door closer that is leaking oil be topped up?

No. Modern closers are sealed hydraulic units with no fill port, so once the fluid is out the body has to be replaced. A leaking closer either slams hard enough to split the frame or stops short of the latch, and both damage the opening while defeating the point of a self-closing door.

Why do our closers get so much worse every January in Clarington?

Hydraulic fluid thickens as the temperature drops, so the same closer that felt fine in July becomes slow and stiff in a cold snap and often leaves the door short of the strike. The right answer is seasonal adjustment of sweep, latch speed and backcheck, not winding the spring up so hard that nobody can pull the door open.
