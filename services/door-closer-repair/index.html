A door closer is the cheapest piece of life-safety hardware in a Clarington building and the one most likely to be ignored until it fails. Ontario's Fire Code requires the self-closing devices on fire separations to be maintained in operating condition, and those doors must never be wedged, blocked or altered so they cannot close and latch on their own. That covers a great deal of what we see here — stairwell doors in Bowmanville apartment and condo buildings, corridor and mechanical room doors in schools and retirement residences, unit separations in the Courtice industrial parks, and back-of-house doors in the King Street and King Avenue shops.

We repair and replace surface, concealed and floor closers, size the closer for the door, the swing and the wind pressure the opening actually sees, and repair the frames and doors that closers tear out of. Then we set sweep speed, latch speed and backcheck for cold-weather operation, because Clarington winters are exactly when closers fail people: hydraulic fluid thickens, the door slows, and it stalls short of the strike so the fire door never latches. A closer is a sealed unit — once it is weeping oil there is nothing to top up, and the honest answer is replacement.

FAQs

Door Closer Repair & Replacement — your questions

Our fire door does not latch on its own. How urgent is that in Ontario?

Urgent. Ontario’s Fire Code requires closures in a fire separation to be kept in operating condition, and a door that does not latch by itself is not doing its job: the latch is what holds it shut against the pressure of a fire. It is also one of the most common items an inspector writes up, and it is usually a straightforward repair.

A closer strong enough for winter is too heavy for our older customers. How is that solved?

By sizing it, not by winding it. A closer that is too large gets wound down until the door no longer latches; one that is too small gets wound up until nobody can pull it open. The right size for that leaf, with sweep, latch and backcheck adjusted separately, gives a door that opens with a reasonable push and still latches at minus fifteen. Where a genuinely accessible entrance is needed, that becomes a low-energy operator conversation rather than a spring-tension one.
