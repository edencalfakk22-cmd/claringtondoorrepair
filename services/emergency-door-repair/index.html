Common Clarington door emergency scenarios — and what to do right now

The moment something fails, get us on the phone at (905) 555-0148. Until a Clarington tech arrives, here’s exactly what to do — and what not to do:

1 A deep-freeze night and the deadlatch never catches

This is the Clarington call that lands right at closing time in January. Once the temperature drops well below minus fifteen the hydraulic fluid in the closer thickens, the door drifts shut a few millimetres short of the strike, and the deadlatch never seats. From the sidewalk the shop looks locked. It is not. Storefronts along King Street West, the Highway 2 and Courtice Road plazas and the Clarington Boulevard units all do this on the same cold night.

Do this right now: Before you drive away, pull hard on the outside handle. If the door moves at all the premises are not secured, so stay on site or get someone there, and do not settle for a wedge or a cable looped through the pulls. Leave the closer valves alone: sweep, latch speed and backcheck each control a different part of the swing, and a random turn can dump whatever oil is left in the body. Call (905) 555-0148 and we will get it latching and locking tonight.

2 Overnight break-in: the jamb is peeled and the door will not shut

Pried entrances are our second-most-common night call, and the damage is almost never confined to the lock. A bar worked into the lock edge bends the jamb, tears the strike out of the frame and twists the hinges, so the door no longer closes square even when the cylinder still turns. We see it on downtown Bowmanville back doors, on plaza units off Highway 2, and on service and loading doors around Lake Road and the Clarington Energy Business Park.

Do this right now: Photograph everything for the police report and your insurer before anyone tidies up or sweeps. If glass has gone, tape the area off and leave the shards where they fell rather than pulling them out of the frame by hand. Do not shoulder the door back into a bent jamb either, because that turns a strike-and-lock repair into a frame replacement. Call (905) 555-0148 and we will make the opening secure before morning.

3 A fire door that has quietly stopped latching on its own

Stairwell doors in Bowmanville apartment and condo buildings, corridor and mechanical room doors in schools and retirement residences, and unit separations in the Courtice industrial parks all depend on closing and latching unaided. Cold, a weeping closer or a strike knocked out of line stops that happening, and the wedge usually appears within the week. Ontario's Fire Code requires those self-closing devices to be kept in operating condition, and a door in a fire separation must not be held open or altered so it cannot close and latch.

Do this right now: Treat this as a code compliance problem rather than a convenience one. Take the wedge or hold-open out, log the fault in your fire safety records, keep the opening clear, and have it repaired the same day instead of living with it until the next inspection. If traffic flow is the real reason it keeps getting propped, ask us about an alarm-linked hold-open device, which is the legitimate way to solve it. Call (905) 555-0148.

4 Wind takes a door on an open lot north of the 401

There is nothing between a farm shop outside Enniskillen or Tyrone and the wind crossing the fields. A gust catches the leaf, throws it past its stop, and rips the closer arm, the top hinge or the strike clean out of the jamb, leaving the door swinging in a storm and the building wide open. Exposed loading and service doors do the same, and so do entry doors on the newer Northglen and Brookhill lots before the fences and trees fill in.

Do this right now: Keep everyone out of the swing path first, because a loose steel door in a gust does real damage to people and vehicles. Do not rope or bungee it shut and hope it holds overnight, and do not drive the arm back into the same torn-out screw holes, which strips what solid material is left. If you can close the building off from the inside, do that and leave the door for us. Call (905) 555-0148 with your concession road and the nearest crossroad so dispatch can route the truck.

What counts as a door emergency?

Given Clarington's heritage king street storefronts in downtown bowmanville and courtice growth along highway 2 and the new go corridor, don't wait for business hours if you have any of these:

  • The deadlatch will not seat, so the premises look locked from the street but are not
  • A jamb pried or peeled at the lock edge after a break-in, with the door no longer closing square
  • A fire separation or stairwell door that will not close and latch unaided, or that is being held open with a wedge
  • An exit or panic device that will not release from inside an occupied building
  • A closer arm, hinge or strike torn out of the frame by wind on an exposed lot
  • A door seized or iced into its opening with people needing to get in or out

Emergency FAQs

Door emergency — fast answers

It is well below freezing and our Bowmanville shop will not lock. Does that really warrant an after-hours call?

Yes. A deadlatch that is not seating leaves the building open even though the door looks shut, and most commercial policies expect the premises to be properly secured overnight. We come out, correct the alignment and the strike so the door latches and locks in the cold, and if a part has to come in the next morning we secure the opening properly in the meantime. Call (905) 555-0148 and tell dispatch the door will not lock.

Can you secure the opening tonight if the right part is not on the truck?

That is normally how a night call ends. Securing the building comes first and the permanent repair follows in daylight once the hardware arrives. We carry common deadlatches, cylinders, closers, hinges and pivots, so a good share of Clarington calls finish on the spot, and we tell you honestly which one yours is before anything starts.

Our stairwell fire door has stopped latching. Can we wedge it until Monday?

No. Ontario's Fire Code requires the self-closing devices on fire separations to be kept in operating condition, and a door in a separation must not be wedged, blocked or altered so it cannot close and latch. A propped stairwell door is one of the most common findings written up on an inspection, and it is the difference between a contained fire and one that runs the length of a corridor. Have it repaired the same day.

Do you actually come out to the hamlets and concession roads after dark?

We do. Orono, Newtonville, Hampton, Enniskillen, Tyrone, Maple Grove and Mitchell Corners are ordinary calls for us rather than a special trip, and we load hinges, closers, strikes and lock hardware before heading north of the 401 so a rural door does not turn into a second visit. Give us the concession road and the nearest crossroad and we will find you.

The glass in our entrance door was smashed. What should we do before you arrive?

Keep people well back and leave the shards where they are. Glass still hanging in a door frame lets go without warning, and pulling it out by hand is how people get badly cut. Photograph the damage for your insurer first, then let us clear the frame, secure the opening and re-glaze it. Glazing in an entrance door has to be tempered or laminated safety glass, so plain glass is never an acceptable replacement.
