It sticks and it will not latch are the two most common calls we get in Clarington, and they are usually the same problem seen in two different seasons. Humid air off Lake Ontario swells wood doors and older frames through the summer; a January cold snap contracts everything, slows the closer and stops the deadlatch reaching the strike. In between, freeze-thaw keeps nudging the frame. In the newer subdivisions around Northglen, Brookhill and Port Darlington there is a third cause: a house that is still settling in its first couple of years will push a door out of square all on its own.

We diagnose the whole assembly — hinges, pivots, frame square, strike position, threshold height and closer settings — and correct the cause rather than moving the strike plate and hoping. Then we deal with the bottom of the door, which on an exposed Clarington lot is where the winter actually gets in: thresholds, sweeps, bottom seals, weatherstripping and astragals, fitted to the door's real gap along its full width rather than to a catalogue number. On rural properties out past Enniskillen, Hampton and Newtonville, where there is nothing to break the wind coming off the fields, that bottom seal is doing more work than anything else in the opening.

FAQs

Door Alignment, Thresholds & Weatherstripping — your questions

Our door sticks in August and rattles in February. Is that two problems?

One. Wood and composite doors take on moisture through a humid summer off the lake and give it back over winter, so the same leaf is fat in August and slim in February. Trimming it to clear in August leaves you with a draught in February. We set the clearance for the range the door genuinely moves through and let the seals cover the difference.

Snow drifts in under our entrance door. Is that the threshold?

Usually the sweep first, the threshold second, and sometimes a leaf sitting high at one end. On an exposed lot the wind drives snow horizontally, so a gap that would never matter on a sheltered street becomes a drift on the mat. We measure the clearance at both ends of the opening, replace the sweep and sill where they are worn, and correct the door if it is hanging unevenly.

Our house is only a few years old and the doors already will not latch. Is that normal?

Common, and fixable. New builds in Northglen, Brookhill and down toward Port Darlington settle through their first couple of years, and builder-grade hinges and strikes are the first things to give when a frame moves a few millimetres. It is less a defect in the doors than a house that has finished moving. Re-hanging the leaves and relocating the strikes normally sorts a whole house in one visit.
