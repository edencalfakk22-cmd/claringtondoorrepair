FAQs

Hinge & Pivot Replacement — your questions

The hinge screws keep pulling out of an old wooden frame. Can that be fixed for good?

Yes, though not with longer screws alone. Once a hole is chewed out, the answer is solid backing behind the jamb, a repair to the timber itself, or a continuous hinge that carries the load down the whole edge instead of at three points. We also look at why that load got there: a leaf that had dropped, or a closer wound too tight, puts a prying force on the top hinge every single time somebody opens the door.

Is a continuous hinge worth it on a busy commercial door?

On a high-cycle opening, generally yes. It spreads the weight down the full height of the leaf, so there are no three points quietly working themselves loose, and it keeps the door hanging true far longer between visits. On a back door that gets used twice a day, ordinary heavy butt hinges are perfectly adequate and cost less.

We had the door lifted last year and it has dropped again. Did the repair fail?

Sometimes, but more often the pivot was renewed while the reason it wore out was left alone. Grit and brine in the bottom pocket grind a new pivot down inside a season, and a frame that is spreading keeps loading it sideways. We clean and repack the pocket, check the frame is properly anchored, and where a leaf keeps dropping we look at the grade of hinge or pivot rather than fitting the same part again.
