What has actually failed is usually the bottom pivot, the top corner of the door, or a frame the building has pushed out of square. We take the door out, replace the worn pivots and bearings, re-square the corners, reset the threshold and put the closer back to a sensible setting — then confirm the door latches and locks unaided. Because Clarington entrances take salted slush and grit straight into the pivot pocket every winter, we clean and repack that pocket as part of the repair rather than dropping new hardware into the same abrasive mess.

FAQs

Storefront & Aluminum Door Repair — your questions

Why does our downtown Bowmanville storefront door drag on the threshold every winter?

Salt, sand and slush get walked into the bottom pivot and grind it out, and freeze-thaw movement shifts the frame at the same time. The door then sits low at the lock corner and catches on the threshold. Replacing and repacking the pivot and re-squaring the door fixes it properly; adjusting the closer only hides it for a few weeks.

Can a bent aluminum storefront door be repaired, or does it need replacing?

Most can be repaired. We straighten and reinforce corners, replace stiles, rails and pivot hardware, and re-hang the door. Replacement is only worth it when the extrusion itself is torn or the frame is beyond straightening, and we tell you which it is before any work starts.

Our Courtice plaza unit is almost new and the door already will not latch — why?

New commercial entrances usually leave the factory with a generic closer setting that was never adjusted for an Ontario winter, and a plaza door cycling hundreds of times a day wears a bottom pivot quickly. We correct the alignment, set the closer for cold-weather operation and check the strike, which is normally all a young door needs.
