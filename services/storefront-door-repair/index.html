What has actually failed is usually the bottom pivot, the top corner of the door, or a frame the building has pushed out of square. Downtown Bowmanville makes that harder still, because a lot of those aluminum entrances were retrofitted into masonry openings that have been settling since the 1800s and were never square to begin with. We take the door out, replace the worn pivots and bearings, re-square the corners, reset the threshold and put the closer back to a sensible setting — then confirm the door latches and locks unaided. Because Clarington entrances take salted slush and grit straight into the pivot pocket every winter, we clean and repack that pocket as part of the repair rather than dropping new hardware into the same abrasive mess.

FAQs

Shop Front & Aluminum Door Repair — your questions

Our King Street doorway is not square and never has been. Can a modern door work in it?

Yes, but not straight out of the box. A century-old masonry opening is usually out by several millimetres at the head or one jamb, and a stock door dropped into it binds at one corner within a season. The work is packing and shimming the frame to the wall it genuinely sits in, then hanging the leaf to that frame. Where original hardware can be saved and reused, we save it.

Our Courtice unit is nearly new and the door already will not latch. Why?

Two reasons, and they compound. Builder-grade closers leave the factory on a default setting that has nothing to do with an Ontario January, and a plaza door cycling hundreds of times a day goes through a bottom pivot within its first couple of seasons. Neither is exactly a defect; both are things nobody adjusted. Setting the closer properly and renewing the pivot usually sorts it.

Our recessed entryway collects water and it freezes overnight. Anything to be done?

Some of it, yes. A deep recessed entrance does not drain the way a flush shop front does, so meltwater pools at the sill and refreezes under the door. Getting the threshold height and slope right, replacing a sweep that has worn flat and clearing the pivot pocket all help, and the door stops being frozen to its own sill in the morning. What we will not do is pretend a seal fixes a drainage problem.
