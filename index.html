Built for Clarington

Clarington properties, Clarington expertise

  • 🔧

    Heritage King Street storefronts in downtown Bowmanville

    Historic Downtown Bowmanville holds close to two hundred businesses in brick buildings that mostly date to the 1800s, with the deep recessed entranceways and tall storefront openings of the period. Most of those entrances are aluminum doors that were retrofitted into masonry openings which have been settling for well over a century, so the frame was never truly square to start with and every winter nudges it a little further. Squaring the door to the opening it actually has — rather than to the catalogue dimension — is what makes a downtown Bowmanville door latch again and stay latched.

  • 🔧

    Courtice growth along Highway 2 and the new GO corridor

    Courtice is one of the fastest-growing communities in Durham Region, and the plazas and commercial units going up along Highway 2, Courtice Road and around the future GO station bring a wave of brand-new aluminum entrances with them. New does not mean trouble-free: builder-grade closers get set at the factory and never adjusted for an Ontario winter, and a plaza door cycling hundreds of times a day chews through a bottom pivot in its first couple of seasons. We service these openings as wear items rather than waiting for the tenant to call on a Saturday night because the unit will not lock.

  • 🔧

    Newcastle Village and Wilmot Creek — doors people can actually open

    Along King Avenue in Newcastle Village and out at the Wilmot Creek adult-lifestyle community, we see the other side of the closer problem. A closer wound tight enough to beat a winter draft becomes a door that an older customer cannot pull open at all, and the usual workaround is a wedge under it, which defeats the closer entirely. The fix is sizing the closer correctly for the door and the opening, then setting sweep, latch speed and backcheck so it closes reliably without needing a shoulder behind it.

  • 🔧

    Rural Clarington — detached homes, farm shops and exposed lots

    North of the 401, Clarington is concession roads and hamlets: Orono, Newtonville, Hampton, Enniskillen, Tyrone, Maple Grove, Mitchell Corners. Entry doors, mudrooms, shops and outbuildings out here sit on open lots with nothing to break the wind coming across the fields, so doors get slammed past their stops, hinges pull loose, and gravel dust works into every moving part in the summer. There is no vestibule to catch the weather either, which means the entry door itself is doing all the work. Rural calls are routine for us, and we come with the hardware on the truck rather than promising a second trip.

  • 🔧

    Freeze-thaw and road salt off the 401 and Regional Road 57

    Clarington winters swing back and forth across zero all season, and salt and sand come off the 401, Liberty Street and Regional Road 57 straight into every entrance in the municipality. That grit turns a bottom pivot into grinding paste, corrodes thresholds and sill plates, and works aluminum frames loose as the building moves with the temperature. A door that latched perfectly in November is dragging by the middle of January. We clean and repack the pivot pocket and reset the threshold instead of just cranking the closer harder and calling it done.

  • 🔧

    January cold snaps: closers stiffen and doors stop latching

    Once the temperature drops well below minus fifteen, hydraulic closer fluid thickens, the closer slows, and the door stalls a few millimetres short of the strike. At the same time metal contracts, weatherstripping goes stiff and brittle, and the deadlatch stops catching — so the door looks shut while the premises are not actually locked. Cold also puts real thermal stress on entrance glass, which is why a hairline in a sidelite in December rarely survives to spring. We adjust sweep, latch speed and backcheck for the season and correct the strike so the door latches in the cold, not just in the showroom.

  • 🔧

    Fire doors in Clarington commercial, industrial and apartment buildings

    From the industrial units around the Clarington Energy Business Park in Courtice to apartment and condo stairwells in Bowmanville and the schools and retirement residences across the municipality, fire separations rely on doors that close and latch on their own. Ontario's Fire Code requires those self-closing devices to be kept in operating condition, and doors in a fire separation must never be wedged, blocked or altered so they cannot close and latch unaided. A propped stairwell door or a closer that stops short of the strike is one of the most common findings an inspector writes up — and in our experience the propping stops once the door actually works properly again.

  • 🔧

    New subdivisions settling — and humid summers off the lake

    Clarington is building fast, and new houses in Northglen, Brookhill and down toward Port Darlington move for the first couple of years while everything settles. Doors that were plumb at closing go out of square, latches start missing, and the interior doors bind. Then comes the flip side of the winter: humid air off Lake Ontario in July swells wood doors and older frames, so the same door that rattled loose in February binds in the jamb in August and gets shouldered closed all season. That shouldering is what loosens hinges, splits stops and pulls strike plates. We set the door for the swing it has year-round instead of chasing the season back and forth.

What goes into your estimate

Estimates here get written after the visit. A door in a settled 1800s masonry opening and a door on a windswept lot outside Enniskillen fail differently and cost differently. The usual factors:

  • How square the opening is — Older Bowmanville masonry has moved for a century, and squaring the door to it is most of the job.
  • Wind exposure on the lot — An entrance with open fields in front of it needs heavier hardware and a proper overhead stop.
  • What the salt has reached — Sills, door bottoms and pivot pockets take sand and brine off the 401 and Regional Road 57.
  • Whether the opening is rated — A fire door has to close and latch unaided, which limits what can simply be adjusted away.
  • Access on the day — Rural calls are routine, but a shop door behind a locked yard needs the visit planned.

Clarington FAQs

Your questions, answered

Is this commercial work only, or do you take houses too?

Both. Shop fronts, plaza units, industrial buildings and farm shops make up most of the week, and residential work is mainly entry doors, patio doors and interior doors that stopped latching after a settlement season. Same trucks, same written scope.

Are you licensed and insured?

Insured and WSIB covered, with certificates available before we start. Licensing is the part people get told wrong: Ontario does not issue a provincial trade licence for door repair, so there is no number for anyone in this trade to hold.

Does it cost anything to have somebody come and look?

No. Somebody comes out, works out what actually moved, and leaves a written fixed scope. If the answer is that the door has years left in it, that is what we will tell you, and it still costs nothing.

Our storefront will not lock and we close in an hour. Can anyone come tonight?

Say that when you call (905) 555-0148 and it goes to the top of the list. If we can reach you tonight we will, and if we genuinely cannot we will say so straight away so you can make other arrangements instead of waiting.

Why does every door in the building get worse in January?

Closer fluid thickens, so the door slows and stops short of the strike. Steel contracts and the frame grips a bolt that was already tight. Weatherstripping stiffens and stops sealing. Three separate things arrive in the same cold snap, which is why a door that was merely annoying in November stops latching altogether.

Can a fire door that no longer closes on its own be repaired?

Almost always, and it should be. The Fire Code requires closures in a fire separation to be kept in operating condition and never wedged or altered so they cannot close and latch unaided. Usually the closer is the wrong size, the mount has loosened, or the leaf binds on a frame that moved.

Do you cover rural Clarington, or only Bowmanville and Courtice?

The whole municipality. Orono, Newcastle Village, Newtonville, Hampton, Enniskillen, Tyrone, Maple Grove, Wilmot Creek and Port Darlington are all normal calls, and we load the truck for a rural visit so it does not turn into two.
